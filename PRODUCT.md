## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js (App Router) with React and TypeScript, deployed to Vercel. Specified by the user in the original brief ("make a website in next.js then host on vercel"), matching the pattern used for the sibling trade sites.

## Users

Drivers in Derby and the surrounding villages whose car has stopped, failed to start, or thrown a warning light where it stands: a driveway, a work car park, a supermarket bay, a kerb at two in the morning. They are not browsing. They are looking for a number to ring, proof that someone will actually come out at this hour, and reassurance that the person arriving is a real local trader rather than a call centre.

A second, calmer audience books ahead: a service, brake pads, a battery before winter, done at home or at work instead of losing a day to a garage.

## Product Purpose

A single page that turns a Google Maps listing with no website into something a stranded driver can trust in about ten seconds. It exists to make one phone call happen. Success is the call, not the scroll.

## Positioning

A mobile mechanic who answers at any hour and has a real workshop behind him. AD Motors Mobile Services 24/7 is the mobile arm of AD Motors Limited at 41-45 Cameron Road, Derby, so a job that cannot be finished at the roadside has somewhere to go. Confirmed by the user, 20 August 2026. Most mobile mechanics are a van and nothing else; most garages close at six.

## Operating Context

The visitor is outdoors, often in bad light, on a phone, on mobile data, sometimes with a passenger waiting and a car blocking a lane. Thumb reach and one visible phone number matter more than anything else on the page. The competing options in the visitor's head are a national recovery membership they may not hold, a main dealer that is shut, and a stranger from a Facebook group.

## Brand Commitments

The name carries "24/7" inside it, so availability is the identity, not a feature line. The trading name is written as the Google profile writes it.

The client's standing visual preference is the house dark-professional canon: graphite ground, hairline rules as the only structural device, one rationed chromatic accent, wide-stretched Archivo caps against plain sentence-case body, flat depth. Recorded as a brand commitment on 20 August 2026, when a fully built expressive direction (a Midland Railway signal box, which the client had chosen from the roll) was shown and rejected with "something more professional please". Canon is the commitment for this site; it is not to be reinterpreted or given a smuggled quirk.

The accent for this site is copper, chosen by the client on 20 August 2026. Accent is the whole of the divergence between the sibling sites, and copper is the seventh in the register.

No customer reviews are published anywhere reachable: the Google profile shows a "Write a review" prompt and no rating, Yell has no listing for the mobile arm, and the reviews that exist online belong to AD Motors Limited's car sales side and are not this business's to quote.

## Product Principles

1. The phone number is the product. Every viewport keeps a way to ring visible or one thumb-move away.
2. Publish only what a source states. An empty section beats a plausible sentence.
3. Answer the hour question first. "Open 24 hours" is the single fact that separates this from every other listing in the results.
4. The workshop behind the van is the credibility, and it is real, so say it plainly and once.
5. Built for a phone held outdoors at night: large targets, high contrast, no motion that delays the number.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Outdoor phone use in poor light sets the bar: WCAG AA contrast at minimum on every text size actually used, tap targets no smaller than 44px, the primary call action reachable without scrolling on a 390px viewport, and full function with reduced motion.
